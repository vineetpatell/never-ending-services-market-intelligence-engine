Backtesting Methodology Specification
Document ID: NES-BT-012 | Version: 1.0
Build approach: Cline-assisted development  Local-first Windows application
Scope: Personal research, paper trading and insurance document assistance
Assumptions, metrics, bias controls and validation
1. Purpose
Define the minimum methodology for historical strategy testing so results are reproducible and do not accidentally overstate
performance.
2. Required inputs
  Strategy name/version and parameters.
  Symbol or universe and exchange.
  Start/end dates and bar interval.
  Data source, timestamp convention and adjustment policy.
  Starting capital and position-sizing method.
  Fees, taxes and slippage assumptions where relevant.
  Order timing and fill assumptions.
  Benchmark and risk-free assumptions if used.
 
3. Methodology rules

 Avoid look-ahead bias: a decision may only use information available at that time.
  Avoid survivorship bias where universe history is available and relevant.
  Document missing bars, stale values and corporate-action treatment.
  Make signal timing and fill assumptions explicit.
  Use out-of-sample or walk-forward evaluation when appropriate.
  Do not tune parameters on the final evaluation period without disclosure.
  Separate training, validation and test periods for ML workflows.
 
4. Required outputs
 Metric
Definition requirement
Total return
Specify cash flows, fees and calculation period.
Benchmark return
Name benchmark and align periods.
Maximum drawdown
Document equity curve basis and peak-to-trough method.
Trade count
Define what counts as an opened/closed trade.
Win rate
Define winning trade and handling of open positions.
Costs
Show fee and slippage assumptions.
Equity curve
State whether returns are realised, mark-to-market or both.
5. Validation tests
  Use tiny hand-calculated datasets to verify trade timing and metrics.
  Test missing bars, gaps, duplicate bars and extreme values.
  Test no-trade and single-trade scenarios.
  Check that the same data and parameters reproduce the same result.
  Compare a simple baseline strategy against expected output.
 
6. Presentation rules
  Label results historical, not predictive.
  Show test period, source, assumptions and warnings near the results.
  Do not use animated count-ups to obscure negative performance.
  Do not present model confidence as calibrated unless calibration has been evaluated.
 
Backtest results are simulations. They are not proof of future performance and are not a guarantee of returns.
